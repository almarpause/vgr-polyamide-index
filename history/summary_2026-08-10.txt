Colombia is the most expensive place on earth to buy this t-shirt: €16.57, four times what it costs in India. The garment is identical — made in Turkey, shipped from Spain — so the gap is not the product. It is what each market adds on top: tariff, tax, freight, and the margin Zara can hold at full price.

Spain anchors the index at €7.95 and runs the thinnest margin by design; the money is made further out. The United States clears close to €8 of margin on a shirt that lands for under three euros, and pays a 32% man-made-fibre duty it only faces because Turkey has no trade deal with Washington.

India is the outlier the other way. At ₹450 the shelf price does not cover landed cost — the single market in the index selling this shirt at a loss. This is the baseline reading; from next month we track who moves, in the top and in the middle.
